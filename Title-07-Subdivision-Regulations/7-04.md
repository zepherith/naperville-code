### 7-4-1: STANDARD OF DESIGN AND REQUIRED IMPROVEMENTS

The following requirements as set forth in this Chapter are hereby adopted as the minimum standards of design of a subdivision and of the improvements required to be constructed or installed therein. Standards and practices of the City are included in the "Naperville Design Manual For Public Improvements", as amended from time to time, on file at the office of the City Engineer. (Ord. 02-137, 6-4-2002)

### 7-4-2: STREETS

1\. Street Right-of-way: The minimum right-of-way (ROW) of a street, alley, arterial and lesser roadways shall be as hereinafter indicated and as recorded on the official plan as adopted by the City Council, including any and all amendments made from time to time and shall not be less than the following, unless otherwise reduced by the City Engineer on a site specific basis dependent on actual right-of-way needs and planned right-of-way improvements: (Ord. 06-217, 9-19-2006)

1.1. Expressway: Any highway designated by the State and local government for purposes of cross country traffic, including thoroughfares presently designated and all thoroughfares designated for future improvement by proper governmental authorities. Complete access control will be provided.

Minimum ROW: Two hundred (200) feet.

1.2. Major Arterial: These streets are used primarily to carry the heavier traffic pattern providing continuity throughout the contiguous urban area. Access control will be maintained towards limiting access to intersections with other roads.

Minimum ROW: One hundred twenty (120) feet.

1.3. Minor Arterial: A street used primarily for intersections of sections of the City and deemed desirable for construction of other public utilities within the street right-of-way.

Minimum ROW: One hundred (100) feet. (Ord. 01-209, 10-2-2001)

1.4. Collector Street: A street used primarily to provide ready collection of traffic from residential areas and to convey this traffic to the major arterial and highway system. Even though this street may carry some through traffic, its primary function is to feed traffic to the arterial streets and to provide local access.

Minimum ROW: Eighty (80) feet.

A lesser ROW may be considered if as part of an integrated plan the public requirements could be met with the lesser ROW. Said lesser ROW requirement may be administratively approved by the City Engineer.

1.5. Neighborhood Connector Street: The primary function is to connect residential and local streets within a neighborhood to collector streets and to the arterial street network. All neighborhoods have at least one neighborhood connector street, and many have two (2) or more.

Where a designated neighborhood connector street fronts a school, a park, or a church site, the minimum ROW shall be eighty (80) feet for the segment of the neighborhood connector between the arterial/ collector street and the next street intersection; otherwise, the minimum ROW shall be sixty-six (66) feet.

Where a designated neighborhood connector street intersects an arterial or collector street as identified in the master thoroughfare plan, the minimum ROW shall be eighty (80) feet for the first block of the neighborhood connector street or for a minimum of three hundred

(300) feet.

A lesser ROW may be considered if as part of an integrated plan the public requirements could be met with the lesser ROW. Said lesser ROW requirement may be administratively approved by the City Engineer.

1.6. Local Streets: The primary function is to serve adjoining property. They shall be arranged to conform to the topography, to discourage use by through traffic and provide access to abutting property.

Minimum ROW: Sixty-six (66) feet.

A lesser ROW may be considered if as part of an integrated plan the public requirements could be met with the lesser ROW. Said lesser ROW requirement may be administratively approved by the City Engineer. (Ord. 06-217, 9-19-2006)

1.7. Cul-De-Sacs: Design will be in accordance with City requirements. The length of cul-de-sacs shall be determined by the degree of access for the purposes of service. Each cul-de-sac shall be considered on its individual merit. The amount of pavement width desirable shall be subject to the recommendation of the Plan Commission.

1.8. Alleys: Minimum ROW: Thirty (30) feet. (Ord. 01-209, 10-2-2001)

### 7-4-3: UTILITIES

1\. Water Supply And Distribution: An adequate water supply and distribution system shall be designed in accordance with Title 8, Chapter 2 of this Code and the "Naperville Design Manual For Public Improvements", as amended from time to time. Fire hydrants shall be spaced: 1) to ensure all points of all buildings (ground level) are within three hundred (300) feet of hose laying length from a hydrant; 2) at intervals not exceeding three hundred (300) feet in commercial, industrial and multi-family (quads and larger) areas.

2\. Sanitary Sewer System: An adequate sanitary sewer system shall be designed in accordance with Title 8, Chapter 2 of this Code and the "Naperville Design Manual For Public Improvements", as amended from time to time. (Ord. 02-137, 6-4-2002)

3\. Electrical Distribution: An electrical distribution system shall be designed in accordance with Title 8, Chapter 1 of this Code. Electrical distribution facilities will be installed by the City, or by a contractor approved by the Naperville Department of Public Utilities, in accordance with Title 8, Chapter 1 of this Code for areas within the municipal limits. The cost of such system shall be paid by the City and/or the subdivider or developer as provided in Title 8, Chapter 1 of this Code. (Ord. 85-238, 12-16-1985; reaffirmed Ord. 89-55, 3-20-1989; amd. Ord. 92-137, 7-21-1992}

4\. Underground Facilities: All facilities constructed to service residential developments shall be placed underground except for special structures and appurtenances to be determined by the City. It is the express purpose of this Subsection to provide no obstructions to the natural features. (Ord. 76-34, 4-20-1976)

5\. Storm Drainage System: An adequate storm drainage system shall be designed in accordance with the storm drainage ordinance of the City and the "Naperville Design Manual For Public Improvements", as amended from time to time. (Ord. 02-137, 6-4-2002}

6\. Easements:

6.1. There shall be dedicated public utility and drainage easements of not less than ten (10) feet in width for poles, wires, conduits, storm or sanitary sewers, gas, cable TV, water or other utility pipes or lines along the rear of each lot, and five (5) feet alongside lot lines where necessary. The easements shall be laid out so that a proper continuity may be developed for such utilities from lot to lot and block to block. Where necessary, in the City's discretion, additional easements shall be provided for utilities and other municipal services in order to provide proper operation and maintenance of such facilities.

6.2. Subject to the provIsIons of Subsections 7-4-3.6.6 and 7-4-3.6.7 of this Section, together with the City's driveway regulations provided in Title 9, Chapter 1, Article I of this Code, driveways may be permitted to encroach within any easement located within any front or corner side yard building setback area parallel to the public right-of-way or public street.

6.3. Except as provided by this Subsection or Section 9-1A-11 of this Code, no buildings, driveways, or structures as defined in Section 6-1-6 of this Code shall be constructed within any interior side or rear lot line easements.

6.4. At the City's discretion, encroachments into a dedicated public utility or any other easement granted to the City may be permitted for the construction of buildings, driveways or structures including brick, stone, or masonry walls or fences; or structures which are wired for electricity, where:

6.4.1. A written encroachment agreement approved by the City Manager is executed by the owner of the property; and (Ord. 95-230, 10-17-1995)

6.4.2. A building permit for the proposed construction is issued by the City's transportation, engineering and development business group. Such permits shall be issued only after approval of complete plans for said construction by the Department of Public Utilities and the Department of Public Works.

6.5. At the City's discretion, encroachments into a dedicated public utility or any other easement granted to the City may be permitted for the construction and installation of all other fences, provided a fence permit for the proposed construction is issued and all required fees are paid to the City's transportation, engineering and development business group. Where any easement is designated for drainage or storm water purposes, a fence permit shall be issued only after approval by the Department of Public Works. (Ord. 02-137, 6-4-2002)

6.6. Any encroachment permitted by this Section shall not be construed as a waiver of any of the other requirements of this Code, including, without limitation, the Zoning Code, the subdivision regulations or the building codes.

6.7. Under no circumstances shall the City be liable to any property owner or third party for any damage to real or personal property or personal injury that may result from or arise out of the construction of any encroachment permitted by this Section. Such permitted encroachments shall be subject to the conditions that:

6.7.1. Said encroaching structure shall not abrogate or nullify the City's rights and interests in and to the easement of record;

6.7.2. The property owners shall retain all of the risks and liability associated with the encroaching structure on a dedicated public utility and drainage easement or any other easement granted to the City, including, without limitation, the risk that in an emergency the City may remove the encroaching structure without any prior notice to the property owners;

6.7.3. The property owners shall be solely responsible for the City's cost to remove the encroaching structure to gain access to any of its public utilities in the easement for any purpose;

6.7.4. The City shall not be responsible for any costs incurred by the property owner to repair or replace the encroaching structure if it is damaged or destroyed by the City for public utility purposes;

6.7.5. The property owners shall hold harmless, indemnify, and defend the City from and against any and all liability, claims, demands, and causes of action arising out of or related to any loss or injury, death, or loss or damage to property resulting from the City's use of any easement for any of its intended purposes or the property owner's construction of or use of the encroaching structure. (Ord. 95-230, 10-17-1995)

### 7-4-4: LAND USE

1\. Blocks: Blocks shall, in general, not exceed one thousand two hundred (1,200) feet in length with a practical maximum being one thousand (1,000) feet. Block lengths and widths shall be coordinated with the development of the land and shall be provided in a manner that will allow the proper traffic flow and pedestrian use for municipal, business, and commercial facilities. Where long blocks are provided for reasons of planning, the City Council may require the construction of a pedestrian crosswalk of not less than five (5) feet in width.

2\. Lots: The size, shape and orientation of lots shall be appropriate for the type of development and use contemplated in accordance with the requirements of this Subsection:

```text
         2.1.       Conformity: All lots shall conform to all of the requirements of the zoning district in
```

which they are situated.

2.2. Residential Lot Width: Residential lots shall not be less than forty (40) feet in width in the entire area between the right-of-way line and the building line. Said requirement shall not be applicable to residential lots proposed for or improved with single-family attached or multiple-family dwellings.

2.3. RD, ORI, And I Districts Lot Width: Lots located in the RD, ORI and I districts shall not be less than sixty-six (66) feet in width from the right-of-way line to the building line.

2.4. Ninety Percent Rule: The minimum lot size of any single-family residential lot that results from resubdividing one or more existing lots shall be equal to or greater than ninety percent (90%) of the mean of all of the single-family residential lots included in the following:

2.4.1. Location: All residential lots located in whole or in part within three hundred (300) feet of any point measured from the property lines of the lot to be resubdivided. Residential lots that are not located within the corporate limits of the City shall be excluded from the calculation of lot sizes within the 300-foot area.

2.4.1.1. If there are any nonresidential lots or lots being used for nonresidential purposes located within the 300-foot area, then those lots shall not be included in determining the ninety percent (90%) calculation.

2.4.1.2. If the 300-foot area includes lots that are zoned or used for single-family detached uses, single-family attached uses, and multi-family uses, or any combination of those uses, then only those lots that are zoned or used for the same use as the proposed resubdivided lots shall be included in determining the ninety percent (90%) calculation.

2.4.2. Residential Lot Defined: For the purposes of this Subsection 7-4-4.2.4, a "residential lot" shall be defined to mean a single residential lot, indicated as such upon a subdivision or assessment plat recorded in the office of the county recorder, within a zoning district in one of the following categories of uses:

2.4.2.1. Single-family detached uses; or

2.4.2.2. Single-family attached uses; or

2.4.2.3. Multi-family uses.

2.4.3. Restrictions: Where the condition imposed by this Subsection 7-4-4.2.4 is either more restrictive or less restrictive than the minimum lot size designation determined by any approved area plan, the more restrictive requirement shall be applicable.

2.4.4. Exemption: The minimum lot size regulations contained herein requiring compliance with the ninety percent (90%) rule shall not apply to any single lot subdivision where no new lots are created.

2.5. Compliance: Nothing in this Section shall be interpreted as relieving any owner from complying with minimum lot width requirements at the building line as determined by the applicable zoning district.
