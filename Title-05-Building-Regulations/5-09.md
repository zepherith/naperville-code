# 5-9 SOIL EROSION AND SEDIMENTATION CONTROL

### 5-9-1: FINDINGS

The City Council finds the following facts to be true:

1\. Excessive quantities of soil may erode from areas undergoing development for certain nonagricultural uses including, but not limited to, the construction of dwelling units, commercial buildings and industrial plants, the building of roads and highways, and the creation of recreational facilities;

2\. The washing, blowing, and falling of eroded soil across and upon roadways endangers the health and safety of users thereof by decreasing vision and reducing traction of road vehicles;

3\. Soil erosion necessitates the costly repairing of gulleys, washed-out fills, and embankments;

4\. Sediment from soil erosion tends to clog sewers and ditches and to pollute and silt rivers, streams, lakes, and reservoirs;

5\. Sediment deposits and high turbidity limit the use of water and waterways for most beneficial purposes, promote the growth of undesirable aquatic weeds, destroy fish and other desirable aquatic life, and are costly and difficult to remove; and

6\. Sediment accumulation often reduces the channel capacity of waterways, resulting in increased chances of flooding at risk to public health and safety.

(Ord. No. 87-19, 2-17-1987)

### 5-9-2: PURPOSE

The purpose of this Chapter is to protect the public health, safety and welfare of the people of the City and the environment in which they live by guiding, regulating and controlling the design, construction, use and maintenance of any development or other activity which disturbs or breaks the topsoil or otherwise results in the movement of earth on land, or removes natural or cultivated vegetation, and which thereby creates the increased possibility of soil erosion, sediment deposits or flooding.

(Ord. 87-19, No. 2-17-1987)

### 5-9-4: GENERAL PRINCIPLES

It is the objective of this Chapter to control soil erosion and sedimentation resulting from development activities in the City. Measures taken to control erosion and sedimentation should be adequate to assure that sediment is not transported from the site by storm events of one-, five-, and ten-year frequency. The following principles shall apply to all development activities within the City and to the preparation of the submissions required under Section 5-9-5 of this Chapter. These principles shall be followed by the site engineer as part of the erosion and sedimentation control plan.

1\. Site development should be planned so that it is consistent with and conforms to the topography and soils of the site so as to prevent and minimize soil erosion. Radical cuts and fills should be avoided in areas of steep slopes. Natural contours should be maintained as much as possible.

2\. Natural vegetation should be retained and protected wherever possible. Areas immediately adjacent to natural watercourses should be left undisturbed wherever possible.

3\. The area of land exposed during site development should be limited to the smallest working area practicable. The period of time during which land is exposed without vegetation should be limited to the shortest period practicable and should be during periods of the least intense rainfall. Stripping and excavation should be minimized. Vegetative cover should be restored at the earliest possible time. Large topsoil stockpiles and land removal of topsoil should be minimized. Topsoil stockpiles should be revegetated during periods of extended use.

4\. Sediment basins, debris basins, desilting basins, or silt traps or filters should be installed and maintained to remove sediment from runoff waters from land undergoing development.

5\. The selection of erosion and sedimentation control measures should be based on the probable frequency of climatic and other events likely to contribute to erosion, and on evaluation of the risks, costs, and benefits involved.

6\. Aesthetics, public health and the requirements of continuing maintenance should be considered in the design and implementation of erosion control facilities and practices as part of an erosion and sedimentation control plan.

7\. Provision should be made to accommodate the increased water runoff caused by changed soil and surface conditions both during and after development. Drainageways should be designed and constructed so that their final gradients and the resultant velocities of water will not create additional erosion on-site or beyond the project limits.

8\. Permanent vegetation and hydraulic structures should be established and installed as soon as practicable during development.

(Ord. No. 87-19, 2-17-1987)

### 5-9-5: EROSION AND SEDIMENTATION CONTROL PERMIT

1\. Permit Required: Except as otherwise provided in this Chapter, no person shall commence or perform any removal, grading, clearing, stripping, excavating, filling or development on any site, parcel, or lot in any zoning district, without first obtaining an erosion and sedimentation control permit from the City.

2\. Exceptions To Permit Requirement: A permit shall not be required for any of the following:

2.1. Development of a site, parcel or lot of less than two (2) acres in any zoning district; provided, that the person responsible for any such development shall implement necessary erosion and sedimentation control measures to satisfy the standards set forth in Subsection 5-9-5.5 of this Section.

2.2. Agricultural use of land, including the implementation of conservation measures included in a farm conservation plan approved by the appropriate soil and water conservation district, and including the construction of hydraulic structures.

2.3. Installation, renovation, or replacement of a septic system to serve an existing dwelling or structure subject to existing City and county ordinances, regulations and policies regarding same.

2.4. Repair work by utilities such as Northern Illinois Gas Company, Illinois Bell Telephone Company, cable television services, and the Naperville Department of Public Utilities, as long as such repair work involves a total ground area of two (2) acres or less and is performed in compliance with Subsection 5-9-5.5 of this Section.

3\. Application For Permit: Application for an erosion and sedimentation control permit shall be made by the owner of the property or their authorized agent to the City Department of Community Development on a form furnished for that purpose. Each application shall bear the names and addresses of the owner and developer of the site and any person retained by the applicant as site engineer together with the name of the individual who will act as the principal contact for the site engineer. Each application shall include the applicant's agreement that any development of parcels shall be in accordance with erosion and sedimentation control plans approved upon issuance of the erosion and sedimentation control permit. Each application shall be accompanied by a fee of three hundred fifty-eight dollars (\$358.00).

4\. Erosion and Sedimentation Control Plans: Each application for an erosion and sedimentation control permit shall be accompanied by the following plans, data and information which, upon approval of the City Engineer and issuance of an erosion and sedimentation control permit, shall constitute the erosion and sedimentation control plans for the parcel.

4.1. A vicinity map for the site for which the permit is sought in sufficient detail to enable easy location in the field of the site, and including the boundary lines and approximate acreage of the site, existing zoning, and a legend and scale. A legal description of the site must also be provided.

4.2. A plan showing:

4.2.1. Existing topography of the site and adjacent land within one hundred (100) feet of the boundaries, drawn at no greater than one-foot contour intervals and clearly portraying the topography and drainage pattern of the area.

4.2.2. The location of existing buildings, temporary structures, utilities, water bodies, flood plains, hydraulic structures, paved areas and other significant natural or human-made features on the site and adjacent land within one hundred (100) feet of the boundary.

4.2.3. A general description of the predominant soil types on the site to a depth of intended excavation, their location, and their limitations for the proposed use.

4.2.4. Proposed use of the site, including present development and planned development and utilization; areas of excavation, grading, and filling; proposed contours, finished grades, and street profiles; provisions for storm drainage, including the control of accelerated erosion, with a drainage area map and computations; kinds and locations of utilities; and areas and acreages proposed to be paved, covered, sodded or seeded, vegetatively stabilized, or left undisturbed.

4.3. A plan showing proposed erosion and sedimentation controls:

4.3.1. All erosion and sedimentation control devices, structures or measures necessary to comply with the general principles of this Chapter and the standards set forth herein. The erosion and sedimentation control plan shall show all such devices, structures or measures to be employed temporarily during development of the parcel and to be employed permanently upon completion of development. These measures shall be shown on the detailed grading plan.

4.3.2. Seeding mixtures and rates, types of sod, method of seedbed preparation, expected seeding dates, type and rate of lime and fertilizer application, and kind and quantity of mulching for both temporary and permanent vegetative control measures.

4.3.3. Provisions for maintenance of erosion and sedimentation control devices, structures or measures, including easements and estimates of the cost of maintenance.

4.3.4. Identification of the person(s) who will have responsibility for maintenance of erosion and sedimentation control devices, structures and measures after development is completed. This identification should be shown on the permit application form.

4.3.5. As a minimum measure, staked hay bales or acceptable alternatives shall be properly placed around the frames of all open lid structures during construction to prevent sediment from clogging sewers and ditches and polluting or silting drainage facilities, watercourses or bodies of water.

4.3.6. The person(s) who will be responsible for the implementation and maintenance of temporary and/or permanent erosion and sedimentation control devices, structures or measures.

4.3.7. That the proposed erosion and sedimentation control plan is consistent with existing or planned development of other parcels which may be affected by, or which may affect, development of the subject parcel.

4.4. The proposed phasing/grading plans of development of the site, including stripping and clearing, rough grading, construction, final grading and landscaping. Documentation on plans should reflect phasing of such development activities and identify a schedule of expected dates on which each phase will begin, the estimated duration of each phase in numbers of days, including the estimated duration of exposure of stripped and cleared areas, and the sequence of stripping and clearing, installation of temporary and permanent erosion and sedimentation control devices, structures or measures, final grading, installation of hydraulic structures, paving streets and parking areas, and establishment of permanent vegetative cover.

5\. Standards: The erosion and sedimentation control plan shall conform to and demonstrate compliance with the standards and requirements contained in Chapter 6, entitled "Design Procedures and Specifications", and Chapter 7, entitled "Runoff", in "Procedures and Standards for Soil Erosion and Sedimentation Control in Northeastern Illinois", prepared by the Northeastern Illinois Erosion and Sedimentation Control Steering Committee and adopted by the Kane/DuPage County Soil and Water Conservation District, dated October, 1981, which standards and requirements are hereby incorporated into and made a part of this Chapter by reference.

6\. Agreement; Waiver: Application for an erosion and sedimentation control permit shall constitute the applicant's agreement to develop the site, parcel or lot in accordance with the requirements of this Chapter and the approved erosion and sedimentation control plan. Specific requirements under this Chapter for the content of the erosion and sedimentation control plan may be waived or modified in a written agreement approved by the City Council if the City Council first determines that development of the parcel in accordance with the erosion and sedimentation control plan submitted will conform with the general principles and purposes of this Chapter.

7\. Bond Requirements: Prior to the issuance of an erosion and sedimentation control permit, the applicant shall submit a corporate surety bond or other financing surety containing substantially the provisions set forth in Section 7-1-6.1 of this Code to guarantee completion of the erosion and sedimentation control plan.

8\. Review and Approval: Each application for an erosion and sedimentation control permit shall be reviewed and acted upon according to the following procedures:

8.1. After receiving an application, DCD shall refer the application to Department of Public Works. Department of Public Works will review each application for an erosion and sedimentation control permit to determine its conformance with the provisions of this Chapter. Department of Public Works may also refer any application which has not yet been so submitted to the appropriate Soil and Water Conservation District and/or any other government or public agency within whose jurisdiction the site is located for review and comment. Any additional fees incurred by such a referral shall be paid by the person applying for the permit. Department of Public Works' approval or disapproval of each application shall be submitted in writing to DCD. If Department of Public Works approves the application, DCD shall issue the permit.

8.2. An erosion and sedimentation control permit may be issued after approval of preliminary engineering plans for the site, parcel or lot and the submission of detailed final engineering plans, all in accordance with the applicable titles of this Code. All work undertaken by the applicant pursuant to the erosion and sedimentation control permit shall be subject to all modifications necessary to comply with the approved final engineering plans for the site, parcel or lot. By undertaking any work pursuant to the erosion and sedimentation control permit prior to approval of final engineering plans by Department of Public Works and any other government or public agency having jurisdiction of the site, parcel or lot, the applicant acknowledges and agrees that all modifications necessary to comply with approved final engineering plans or the requirements of such other government or public agency shall be performed and completed by the applicant at their sole cost and expense.

8.3. Department of Public Works shall approve the issuance of an erosion and sedimentation control permit upon submission, review and approval of an application, erosion and sedimentation control plans, and a bond, all as required by this Chapter. Furthermore, an erosion and sedimentation control permit may be issued only after approval of preliminary engineering plans for the site, parcel or lot and the submission of detailed final engineering plans, all in accordance with the applicable titles of this Code. Issuance of an erosion and sedimentation control permit shall not affect the applicant's obligation to obtain all permits or approvals required by any other government or public agency having jurisdiction of the site, parcel or lot.

9\. Record Drawings: The applicant shall provide Department of Public Works with reproducible record drawings upon completion of the project in compliance with the procedures and standards established by the City Engineer. The City Engineer shall have the authority to establish written procedures and standards for the content, review and approval of record drawings for the purpose of establishing that projects have been completed in conformance with the requirements of this Chapter.

(Ord. No. 87-19, 2-17-87; amd. Ord. No. 92-137, 7-21-1992; Ord. No. 18-145, § 5, 12-18-2018; Ord. No. 22-044, § 2, 5-3-2022)

### 5-9-6: OPERATIONS STANDARDS AND REQUIREMENTS

1\. Applicability: All development, whether subject to the permit requirements of this Chapter or exempt therefrom, shall be subject to the applicable standards and requirements set forth in this Chapter.

2\. Responsibility: The permittee, owner and developer of the site, parcel or lot shall exercise all reasonable care in performing work under this Chapter and any applicable erosion and sedimentation control permit to prevent any direct or indirect damage to other persons or property.

3\. Inspection: The Department of Public Works shall make such periodic inspections of the following described work as may be necessary or appropriate in the discretion of the City Engineer, and shall either approve that portion of the work completed or shall notify the permittee wherein the work fails to comply with the erosion and sedimentation control plan as approved. A copy of the erosion and sedimentation control plan shall be maintained at the site, parcel or lot during progress of the work. The permittee shall notify the Department of Public Works not more than ten (10) nor less than two (2) working days before the anticipated completion of the following listed items for any area or phase of the work:

3.1. Stripping and clearing,

3.2. Rough grading,

3.3. Final grading,

3.4. Final Landscaping.

The City reserves the right to reject the need for formal inspection in writing for these specific items but the Department of Public Works will be solely responsible for making this determination.

4\. City Responsibility: The City shall use its best efforts to inspect and enforce the provisions of this Chapter for the benefit of the people of the City. Nothing in this Chapter shall be construed as creating any special duty to any particular person different from that owed to the public at large.

5\. Special Precautions: If at any stage of development, the City Engineer determines that the nature of the site, parcel or lot is such that further work authorized by an existing permit may cause harm or accelerated erosion to any property, public way, watercourse, drainageway, hydraulic structure or other structure, the City Engineer may require that such reasonable special precautions be taken as are necessary or appropriate to avoid such harm or accelerated erosion. "Special precautions" may include, but are not limited to, one or more of the following requirements: a more level exposed slope, construction of additional drainage structures, berms, terracing, compaction, cribbing, installation of plant materials for erosion control, other temporary structures, implementation of recommendations of a soils engineer and/or engineering geologist, or completion of work in stages so as to insure completion of protective measures or devices by specified dates.

6\. Amendment of Plans: A permittee may request amendments to erosion and sedimentation 6) control plans by submitting amended plans in accordance with the requirements of this Chapter. The City shall process and approve or disapprove the proposed amendments in accordance with the provisions for review and approval of plans in this Chapter.

7\. Expiration of Permit: Erosion and sedimentation control permits shall be valid for one year unless otherwise stated in a written agreement between the permittee and the City in accordance with Section 5-9-5.6 of this Chapter. An erosion and sedimentation control permit under which development has commenced may be extended for up to one additional year upon written request made before the expiration date of the permit. Requests for extensions shall be treated in the same manner as original applications.

### 5-9-7: COMPLIANCE; ENFORCEMENT; HEARINGS; APPEALS

1\. Adherence to Plans: It shall be unlawful for any person to commence or perform any removal, grading, clearing, stripping, excavating, filling or development on any parcel subject to this Chapter, or any property for which an erosion and sedimentation control plan has been approved pursuant to this Chapter, except as provided for in such plan.

2\. Continued Compliance With Plans: It shall be unlawful for the owner of any parcel which has been developed in accordance with an erosion and sedimentation control plan approved pursuant to this Chapter, or which is otherwise subject to the provisions of this Chapter, to alter, change or remove any drainageway, final grade, contour line, watercourse, hydraulic structure, temporary structure or erosion and sedimentation control devices, structures or measures in contravention of such erosion and sedimentation control plan or the provisions of this Chapter; or to allow the property to remain in a condition which does not conform to such plan or the provisions of this Chapter.

3\. Enforcement: The Director of the Department of Public Works is hereby authorized and directed to enforce all of the provisions of this Chapter. Upon presentation of proper credentials, the Director of the Department of Public Works and their duly authorized representatives may enter at reasonable times upon any property within the City to investigate or ascertain the condition of the subject property in regard to any aspect regulated by this Chapter.

4\. Duty of Property Owner: It shall be the duty of the owner of any property required to be improved or developed in conformance with an erosion and sedimentation control plan approved pursuant to this Chapter, or who is otherwise subject to the provisions of this Chapter, to bring such property into compliance with said plan within ten (10) days of receipt of notice from the Director of the Department of Public Works that said property does not conform to said plan under which said property was improved or developed, or with the requirements of this Chapter.

5\. Right of Appeal: Any person aggrieved by a determination of the Director of the Department of Public Works that property does not conform to approved erosion and sedimentation control plans for said property may appeal such a determination by serving written notice on the City Manager. The City Manager shall, within ten (10) business days, appoint an Illinois registered professional engineer with experience in storm water management to serve as an impartial hearing officer. The costs of retaining a hearing officer for the appeal process shall be shared equally by the City and the permittee. The hearing officer shall confine their investigation and ruling to a review of the correctness of the Director of the Department of Public Works' determination of compliance with the approved plans. The hearing officer shall not have the authority to alter or change any such approved plans or to allow a variance from full compliance with such plans.

The hearing officer shall hold a hearing on the appeal, which may be continued from time to time. The hearing officer must convene the hearing within ten (10) business days from the date on which the City Manager appoints the hearing officer. At said hearing the permittee, the City, and any other interested person, may present any relevant evidence, testimony or argument on the question under consideration. At the conclusion of such hearing, the hearing officer shall, within ten (10) business days, make a written report to the City Manager of their findings of facts and recommendations. The hearing officer shall submit a copy of their written report to the permittee and the Director of the Department of Public Works or their respective representatives at the hearing. If additional time is required before convening the hearing or for completing the report after the hearing is concluded, and the hearing officer, the permittee and the Director of the Department of Public Works or their representative agree, such additional time shall be allowed. It shall then be the responsibility of the City Manager to review the hearing officer's findings of facts and recommendations and accept, accept with modifications, or reject the same. The recommendations and findings of fact of the hearing officer shall not be binding on the permittee, the City, or any City Department, officer or employee until received and accepted or rejected by the City Manager. The decision of the City Manager shall be a final administrative decision and subject to review in the manner provided by law.

6\. Appeal of Hearing Officer's Decision: Any appeal of the City Manager's decision accepting, accepting with modification, or rejecting the findings of facts and recommendations of the hearing officer, shall be heard by the Circuit Court of DuPage County, Illinois, by writ of certiorari. Any such appeal shall be filed with the Clerk of the Circuit Court of DuPage County no more than thirty-five (35) days after the City Manager's acceptance or rejection of the hearing officer's findings of facts and recommendation.

7\. Stop-Work Order: In addition to any other remedies available to the City, a stop-work order may be issued in the event any person violates the terms of an erosion and sedimentation control permit or the provisions of this Chapter.

The Director of the Department of Public Works shall have the power, by the issuance of a stop-work order, to order all work authorized pursuant to an erosion and sedimentation control permit or otherwise governed by this Chapter stopped when such work is being done in violation of this Chapter. Such work shall not be resumed after the issuance of such order except on the written permission of the Director issuing the stop-work order, provided that if the stop order is an oral one, it shall be followed by a written stop order within an hour. Such written stop order may be served by any police officer or other person authorized to serve summons. The stop-work order shall be deemed served upon the permittee, owner or developer when delivered to the permittee, owner, developer, site engineer or their officers or agents. The stop-work order shall also be deemed served when posted upon the site at or near the point of principal access to the site.

### 5-9-8: ACCEPTANCE OR APPROVAL OF IMPROVEMENTS

1\. Acceptance of Public Improvements: Any permanent erosion and sedimentation control devices, structures or measures which are intended to be conveyed to the City shall be accepted by the City in accordance with the procedures for the acceptance of public improvements provided in Title 7 of this Code, but only after submission, review and approval of record drawings in accordance with Section 5-9-5.9 hereof.

2\. Maintenance Bond for Public Improvements: The permittee shall also be required, as a condition precedent to acceptance of improvements by the City, to submit a maintenance bond in a form approved by the Corporation Counsel, with good and sufficient sureties, guaranteeing the City against defects in materials, construction and workmanship. Such bond shall be in effect for a period of one year after acceptance of the improvements and shall be in the amount of ten percent (10%) of the estimated cost of construction of such improvements.

3\. Conveyance of Public Improvements: The permittee shall also be required, as a condition precedent to acceptance of such improvements by the City, to convey such improvements to the City, free and clear of all liens and encumbrances, by a duly executed bill of sale in a form approved by the Corporation Counsel.

4\. Approval of Private Improvements: Any permanent erosion and sedimentation control devices, structures or measures which will be under private ownership or control shall be subject to approval of the City Engineer as finally constructed, but only after submission, review and approval of record drawings in accordance with Section 5-9-5.9 hereof.

### 5-9-9: BUILDING AND OCCUPANCY PERMITS

1\. Building Permits: No building permits shall be issued for construction, alteration or remodeling of any building or structure unless all erosion and sedimentation control devices, structures or measures required under this Chapter or an applicable erosion and sedimentation control plan have been constructed and function in accordance with the intent of this Chapter or such plan. Such erosion and sedimentation control devices, structures or measures shall be deemed functional upon receipt of a written statement from an Illinois registered professional engineer that such devices, structures or measures function in accordance with the intent of this Chapter or such plan, provided that the City Engineer concurs in such statement.

2\. Occupancy Permits: No occupancy permit of any kind shall be issued for any building or structure on any site, parcel or lot subject to this Chapter or an applicable erosion and sedimentation control plan unless all erosion and sedimentation control devices, structures or measures have been accepted or approved pursuant to Section 5-9-8 of this Chapter except as provided in Subsection 3 hereof.

3\. Retention and Detention Improvements: A temporary occupancy permit may be issued for a period of up to one year for a building or structure on a site, parcel or lot subject to this Chapter or an applicable erosion and sedimentation control plan if the following requirements have been met:

3.1. If all dry bottom storm water storage areas, wet bottom storm water storage areas, berms, swales, and other similar improvements have been found to be functional as provided herein, and have been fine graded and seeded or sodded in accordance with the approved plans for such improvements. Provided that no final occupancy permits shall be issued until sufficient turf or other acceptable vegetation has been established on such improvements and record drawings thereof have been submitted and approved in accordance with Section 5-9-5.9 of this Chapter.

3.2. All other erosion and sedimentation control devices, structures or measures have been accepted or approved pursuant to Section 5-9-8 of this Chapter.

### 5-9-10: CONFLICT

In the event of any conflict between the provisions of this Chapter and any other provisions of this Code, the more stringent requirement shall be deemed controlling.
