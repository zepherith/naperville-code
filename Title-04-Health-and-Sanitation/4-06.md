# 4-6 HEAT AND HEATING FACILITIES

### 4-6-1: DUTY TO FURNISH HEAT

Every dwelling unit or rooming unit shall have heating facilities which are properly installed, and maintained in a safe and good working condition and are capable of safely and adequately heating all habitable rooms, bathrooms and water closet compartments to a temperature of sixty-eight degrees (68 2) Fahrenheit at a distance of two (2) feet to five (5) feet above floor level when the outside temperature is a minimum ten degrees (10 2) Fahrenheit.

Every owner or operator who owns or operates any dwelling or rooming house in which the heat is furnished from a heating plant used in common for the purpose of heating the various dwelling units or rooming units therein, and such heating plant is under the control or supervision of such owner or operator, shall furnish heat to such dwelling units or rooming units from October 1 of each year to May 1 of the succeeding year. (Ord. 94-35, 3-15-94)

### 4-6-2: MINIMUM HEAT REQUIREMENTS

A minimum temperature of sixty-eight degrees (68°) Fahrenheit from six thirty o'clock (6:30) a.m. to eleven o'clock (11 :00) p.m. and sixty-three degrees (63°) Fahrenheit from eleven o'clock (11 :00) p.m. to six thirty o'clock (6:30) a.m. averaged through any dwelling unit or rooming unit shall be maintained without such undue restriction of ventilation as to interfere with proper healthful conditions. When the outdoor temperature is below the outdoor design temperature stated in Section 4-6-1, the owner or operator shall not be required to maintain the minimum room temperatures, provided the heating system is operating at full capacity, with supply valves and dampers in a full open position. (Ord. 94-35, 3-15-94)

### 4-6-3: MANNER OF DETERMINING TEMPERATURES

For the purpose of determining whether the temperature in any dwelling unit or rooming unit heated as required by Sections 4-6-1 and 4-6-2 of this Code has been attained, temperature readings shall be made under the supervision of the Director of Community Development by means of a standard Fahrenheit/Centigrade thermometer approved by the Director of Community Development in not less than two (2) separate rooms in such dwelling unit, or if the dwelling unit consists of only one room and in the case of a rooming house, then in two (2) opposite parts of the room as near the extremes as practicable, such thermometer to be placed at a point not less than two (2) feet nor more than five (5) feet above the floor of any such room or dwelling unit, and at least four (4) feet away from any outside wall. (Ord. 94-35, 3-15-94)

### 4-6-4: IMPROPER HEATING EQUIPMENT

Gas or electric appliances designed primarily for cooking or water heating purposes and portable heating equipment employing flame and the use of liquid or coal shall not be considered as heating facilities within the meaning of this Chapter 6. (Ord. 94-35, 3-15-94)

### 4-6-5: LOCATION OF HEATING EQUIPMENT

Heating plants designed or intended to serve one or more dwelling units shall not be located in a habitable room. (Ord. 94-35, 3-15-94)

### 4-6-6: ENFORCEMENT; PENALTY

The Director of Community Development shall have primary responsibility for enforcement of this Chapter. Citizens' complaints will only be addressed during City office hours. The owner or operator of any dwelling unit shall repair or replace any heating system not meeting the standards of this Chapter within twenty-four (24) hours of being so notified, and after the first twenty-four (24) hours shall provide, at the owner or operator's own expense, local hotel or motel accommodations for the tenant(s) or occupant(s) until the heating system is repaired or replaced. The owner and operator of any dwelling unit or rooming unit which does not meet the standards set forth in this Chapter shall also be subject to fines and penalties of not less than fifty dollars ($50.00) nor more than five hundred dollars ($500.00) per day. Each day a violation continues shall constitute a separate offense. (Ord. 94-35, 3-15-94)
