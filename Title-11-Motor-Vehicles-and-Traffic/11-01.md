### 11-1-1: ADOPTION OF PROVISIONS OF ILLINOIS VEHICLE CODE BY REFERENCE

1\. The Illinois Vehicle Code, 625 Illinois Compiled Statutes, as amended from time to time, is hereby adopted by reference to be applied within the corporate limits of the City to the extent permitted by law and to the extent that its subject matter is not regulated by this Code or inconsistent with any lawful provisions of this Code.

2\. All references in sections of the Illinois Vehicle Code to "this Code" shall be to sections of the Illinois Vehicle Code. References to a State employee or official may be to a corresponding City employee or official where such reference is applicable or necessary. Any provisions of the Illinois Vehicle Code adopted herein may be cited by placing "11-1 :" before the appropriate Illinois Vehicle Code Section, i. e., 11-1 :11 -501, Naperville Municipal Code.

3\. The burden of proof for any offense classified as a "petty" offense or "business" offense in the Illinois Vehicle Code adopted herein shall be by a preponderance of the evidence. Offenses classified therein as "misdemeanor" offenses shall be proven beyond a reasonable doubt. (Ord.

No. 92-56, 4-7-1992)

### 11-1-2: PENALTIES AND RELATED PROVISIONS

1\. A violation of any provision of this Title unless otherwise specified or of any offenses classified as "petty" offenses under the aforesaid provisions of the Illinois Vehicle Code shall be punishable by a fine of not less than seventy-five dollars ($75.00) nor more than one thousand dollars ($1 ,000.00) for each offense. Offenses classified as "business" or "petty" offenses under the provisions of the Illinois Vehicle Code adopted by reference pursuant to this Chapter shall be punishable as set forth therein.

2\. Offenses classified as "misdemeanors" under the provisions of the Illinois Vehicle Code adopted by reference pursuant to this Chapter shall be subject to a fine and/or imprisonment for a determinant term according to the following limitations:

2.1. For a class A misdemeanor, imprisonment for any term less than one year and/or a fine of not less than two hundred dollars ($200.00) and not to exceed two thousand five hundred dollars ($2,500.00), except as provided in Subsection 11-1-2.2.4 of this Section.

2.2. For a class B misdemeanor, imprisonment for not more than six (6) months and/or a fine of not less than one hundred dollars ($100.00) and not to exceed one thousand five hundred dollars ($1 ,500.00).

2.3. For a class C misdemeanor, imprisonment for not more than thirty (30) days and/or a fine of not less than one hundred dollars ($100.00) and not to exceed one thousand five hundred dollars ($1 ,500.00).

2.4. The court, upon making a finding of guilty for any offense under Section 11-1 :11-501 of this Code, in addition to any sentencing alternative elected by the court, shall impose a fine of not less than seven hundred fifty dollars ($750.00) nor more than two thousand five hundred dollars ($2 ,500.00) for each such offense.

3\. The court, in imposing any fine as provided in this Section, shall consider the financial resources and future ability of the offender to pay the fine, and whether the fine will prevent the offender from making court ordered restitution or reparation to the victim of the offense, if any. (Ord. No. 92-56, 4-7-1992; Ord. No. 99-81, 5-18-1999; Ord. No. 09-067, 6-2-2009)

### 11-1-3: OBEDIENCE TO POLICE, FIREMEN

1\. Police: Members of the Police Department and special police assigned to traffic duty are hereby authorized to direct all traffic in accordance with the provisions of this Title, or in emergencies as public safety or convenience may require, and it shall be unlawful for any person to fail or refuse to comply with any lawful order, signal or direction of a police officer. Except in case of emergency, it shall be unlawful for any person not authorized by law to direct or attempt to direct traffic.

2\. Scene Of Fire: The Fire Department officer in command, or any firefighter designated by them, may exercise the power and authority of a police officer in directing traffic at the scene of any fire or where the Fire Department has responded to an emergency call for so long as Fire Department equipment is on the scene in the absence of or in assisting the police.

3\. Impersonation: It shall be unlawful for any unauthorized person to identify or represent themselves to be a police officer or firefighter or to wear any part of a uniform normally assigned to City police officers or firefighters. (1960 Code, §§ 13.102, 13.103; 1984 Code; Ord. No. 22-044, § 2, 5-3-2022)

### 11-1-4: SIGNS, SIGNALS, AND PAVEMENT MARKINGS

1\. Installation And Maintenance: The City Manager shall make or cause to be made investigation, from time to time, of the traffic and other conditions affecting the use of the streets and public ways in the City to determine the type and location of such warning signs and pavement markings as are requ ired by this Title, or that said City Manager may deem advisable, to give to the public the information, directions, prohibitions or warnings for obedience to the regulations in effect under this Title pertaining to traffic upon such street or public way and for the protection and safety of persons and property. Subject to the prior approval thereof by the City Council, the City Manager is authorized and directed to cause such signs, signals, and pavement markings to be installed and maintained.

2\. Signage:

2.1. All-Way Stop Signs: The placement and erection of all-way stop signs at intersections involving collector and arterial streets, as identified in the City's master thoroughfare plan, shall conform with the regular MUTCD warrant. Intersections involving residential all-way stop signs shall conform with the policy provisions of the City's current residential all-way stop warrant. A copy of the City's current residential all-way stop warrant shall be kept in the City Clerk's office. The provisions of the residential all-way stop warrant shall be administered by the City Engineer. The methodology of the residential all-way stop warrant shall be in substantial conformance with the minimum MUTCD requirements for approach traffic volume, pedestrian volume, adjacent traffic controls, traffic crashes, and other acceptable traffic criteria.

### 11-1-7: TEMPORARY REGULATIONS

The City Manager may impose temporary regulations of the public streets, not to exceed twelve (12) months, including, but not limited to, temporary speed limits, road closures, or other movement regulations whenever the City Manager determines that a special condition or event such as construction, unforeseen event, or emergency exists, so that the standard regulation may create a temporary hazard. The City Manager or their designee shall cause the placement of appropriate signs, which may include the amount of the minimum fine for a violation when workers are present and increased fines for violations of traffic regulations in work zones. The City Manager shall report such temporary regulations to the Mayor and City Council not less than twenty-four

(24) hours or as otherwise practical before such temporary regulations are to take effect. The City Council may rescind any temporary regulation established by the City Manager by majority vote. The temporary regulation shall automatically expire twelve (12) months after establishment unless extended by City Council or otherwise earlier terminated at the direction of the City Manager upon conclusion of the special condition, event, or emergency. Any person violating a temporary regulation shall be subject to fine and penalty as provided in this Code. (Ord. No. 03-85, 3-18-2003; Ord. No. 14-062, § 1, 6-3-2014; Ord. No. 22-044, § 2, 5-3-2022)

### 11-1-8: NO WATERCRAFT PERMITTED WHERE POSTED

No person shall be permitted to operate any watercraft, whether motorized or unmotorized, where signs are visibly posted along the riverbank at locations where such watercraft are prohibited. Any person violating this Section shall be guilty of an ordinance violation and shall be subject to a fine of no more than five hundred dollars ($500.00) for each offense. (Ord. No. 05-091, 6-7-2005)

### 11-1-10: ADMINISTRATIVE HEARINGS SYSTEM

The purpose of this Section is to provide an administrative hearing system within the City that provides a fair and efficient method for the enforcement of designated ordinance violations.

1\. Established:

1.1. There is hereby established and created within the City an administrative hearings system to enforce and adjudicate violations ("violations") of the following provisions of this Code, as the same have been, and may from time to time hereinafter be amended:

1.1.1. Automated Traffic Enforcement Systems (11-1-9, et seq .)

```text
                    1.1 .2.          Towing of Unlawful Vehicles (11-5B-12,  et seq .)
```

1.1.3. Administrative Fee (10-8-1, et seq .)

1.2. The prov1s1ons of 65 Illinois Compiled Statutes 5/1-2.1 and 625 Illinois Compiled Statutes 5/11-208.3 are hereby adopted and incorporated into this Section as if fully set forth herein.

1.3. The provisions of this Section shall apply to administrative adjudication proceedings to the extent that they are not inconsistent with the provisions of this Code that set forth specific procedures for the administrative adjudication of particular Code provisions or any State laws.

2\. Hearing Procedures Nonexclusive: The provisions of this Section shall not preclude the City from using other methods or proceedings to enforce and adjudicate this Code or other ordinances of the City, including, without limitation, the institution of an action in any appropriate court or other tribunal.

3\. Hearing Officer:

3.1. Appointment: One or more qualified Hearing Officers shall be appointed by the City Manager or their designee after a competitive, merit based process and a contract award by the City Council, to perform the functions set forth in this Section.

3.2. Independent Contractor: A Hearing Officer shall be employed as an independent contractor of the City and shall not be considered an employee of the City.

3.3. Authority And Powers: Hearing Officers shall have all powers necessary to conduct fair and impartial hearings including, but not limited to, the power to:

```text
                    3.3 .1.         Preside over the administrative hearing system established herein, as
                                    the adjudicator.

                    3.3.2.          Hold conferences for the settlement or simplification of the issues.
```

3.3.3. Administer oaths and affirmations.

3.3.4. Issue subpoenas to secure the attendance and testimony of witnesses and the production of relevant documents.

3.3.5. Hear testimony and accept evidence that is relevant to the existence of a violation.

```text
                    3.3.6.          Make a final determination of liability.
```

3.3.7. Upon a finding of a violation, impose penalties, fines, and costs consistent with the applicable Code provisions.

```text
                    3.3 .8.         Provide for the accurate recordation of the administrative hearings.
```

3.4. Conflict Of Interest: As an appointed official, the Hearing Officer shall be governed by Section 1-13-9, "Compliance With The State Officials And Employees Ethics Act", of this Code. Additional conflicts provisions may be included in the contract with the Hearing Officer.

4\. Traffic Compliance Administrator: The City Manager or their designee shall appoint a Traffic Compliance Administrator who is authorized to:

4.1. Operate and manage the administrative hearing system.

4.2. Adopt, distribute, and process violation notices and other notices required by this Code and collect money paid as fines and penalties.

4.3. Establish procedures necessary for the prompt, fair and efficient operation of the administrative hearing system.

4.4. Certify copies of the final determinations of violation liability and factual reports, verifying that the final determination of violation liability was issued in accordance with this Chapter and 625 Illinois Compiled Statutes 5/11-208.3.

4.5. Certify reports to the Secretary of State concerning initiation of suspension of driver's licenses in accordance with the provisions of this Chapter and 625 Illinois Compiled Statutes 5/6-306.5.

4.6. Promulgate rules and regulations pertaining to the administrative hearings process, the content of forms and procedures, and the daily operation of the administrative hearing system.

5\. Violations:

5.1. A notice of any violation ("violation notice") shall be issued by the persons authorized under this Code, shall contain information as to the nature of the violation, shall be certified, and shall constitute prima facie evidence of the violation cited.

```text
          5.2.      The violation notice shall specify the date, time, location of the violation,  the ordinance
```

violated, the fine and penalties that may be assessed, the vehicle make and State registration number, when required, and the identification number of the person issuing the citation.

5.3. The violation notice shall state that the payment of the fine and penalty shall constitute a final disposition of the violation.

5.4. The violation notice shall contain information as to the availability of administrative hearings.

5.5. The City shall cause to be retained the original or a facsimile of the violation notice and keep it as a record in the ordinary course of business.

5.6. The violation notice or a copy thereof shall be admissible in any subsequent administrative or judicial proceeding to the extent permitted by law.

6\. Service:

6.1. Service of any violation notice shall be made as follows:

6.1.1. Handing the notice to the registered owner, operator or lessee of the vehicle, if present.

6.1.2. Mailing the notice to the registered owner of the vehicle as recorded with the Secretary of State, the operator or lessee of the vehicle.

6.1.3. Affixing the original or a facsimile of the notice to the offending property.

6.1.4. In accordance with any other provisions of this Code that set forth procedures for the administrative adjudication of particular Code provisions.

6.2. If service of notice is provided by mail for any violation notice or for any hearing notice, said service shall be deemed sent and shall be complete on the date the notice is deposited, postage prepaid, in the United States mail. The counting of any time period as set forth in this Code shall begin to run on the date the notice is deposited, postage prepaid, in the United States mail.

7\. Hearings: All hearings conducted under the administrative hearing system shall be conducted by a Hearing Officer and shall be in accordance with the following rules and procedures:

7.1. Hearings shall be recorded.

```text
          7.2.      Parties may appear pro se or, at their own expense, by an attorney. An attorney who
```

appears on behalf of any party shall file with the Hearing Officer a written appearance on a form provided for such purpose.

7.3. Parties or their counsel may present witnesses and cross examine opposing witnesses.

```text
          7.4.      The Hearing Officer may grant continuances upon a finding of good cause.
```

7.5. All testimony shall be given under oath or affirmation, which the Hearing Officer shall cause to be administered.

7.6. The formal and technical rules of evidence shall not apply. Evidence, including hearsay, may be admitted to the extent determined by the Hearing Officer.

7.7. Each hearing shall culminate in a determination of liability or nonliability by the Hearing Officer or a determination of liability based upon the failure of the defendant to appear at the hearing.

7.8. The Hearing Officer shall, upon a determination of liability, assess fines and penalties in accordance with this Code. In addition, the Hearing Officer shall have the discretion to assess costs upon finding a defendant liable for the charged violation. In no event shall the Hearing Officer have authority to impose a penalty of incarceration.

7.9. The maximum monetary fine imposed under this Code shall be exclusive of costs of enforcement or costs incurred by the City to secure compliance with this Code and City ordinances, and shall not be applicable to cases to enforce the collection of any tax imposed and collected by the City.

8\. Judicial Review: Any final decision by a Hearing Officer that a violation does or does not exist shall constitute a final determination for purposes of judicial review and shall be subject to review under the Illinois Administrative Review Law\*. \* 735 ILCS 5/3-1 01 et seq.

9\. Debt To The City: Any fine, penalty or part of any fine or penalty assessed in accordance with the provisions of this Code and remaining unpaid after the exhaustion of, or the failure to exhaust, administrative procedures under this Code and the conclusion of any judicial review procedures, shall be a debt due and owing the City, and, as such, may be collected in accordance with applicable law.

10\. Enforcement Of Judgments: After expiration of the period that judicial review under the Illinois Administrative Review Law may be sought for a final determination of any violation, unless stayed by a court of competent jurisdiction, the findings, decision, and order of the Hearing Officer may be enforced in the same manner as a judgment entered by a court of competent jurisdiction.

11\. Fine/Penalties:

11.1. Violation submitted to an administrative hearing shall be a civil offense punishable by fine only.

11.2. Fines and penalties for violations shall be as established from time to time by the Mayor and Council. (Ord. No. 08-193, 10-21-2008; Ord. No. 10-117, § 2, 9-7-2010; Ord. No. 10-150, § 1, 12-7-2010; Ord. No. 11-172, § 2, 12-6-2011; Ord. No. 22-044, § 2, 5-3-2022)
